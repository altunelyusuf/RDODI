Software defects discovered after deployment cost dramatically more to correct than defects caught
early in the lifecycle. Across empirical studies the cost ratio between a requirements-phase defect
and a production-phase defect has been observed to range from approximately ten to one hundred,
with the upper bound being most consistent in large systems where downstream rework involves
re-running integration and acceptance tests across multiple subsystems. The economic implication
for management is that prevention investment, measured as a fraction of total project cost, is
recovered many times over in reduced rework cost. This pattern was first systematically reported in
software engineering economics and has since been corroborated in subsequent industry surveys.
The shift-left principle as practised in modern continuous-integration environments operationalises
this finding at higher cadence than was feasible at the time of the original measurement.
